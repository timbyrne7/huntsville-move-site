Why people move here

Huntsville is a defence, space and engineering city first and a lifestyle city second, and that shapes the relocation entirely. Redstone Arsenal and NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center anchor the north and west; Cummings Research Park is the second-largest research park in the country; Mazda Toyota Manufacturing sits out in Limestone County. That means:

  • Your commute matters more than your neighbourhood reputation. Living on the wrong side of the mountain adds half an hour each way.
  • School zones do not follow mailing addresses. An unincorporated Madison County address can carry a "Huntsville" mailing address and sit in the county school system.
  • Alabama property taxes are low enough to change your housing budget. The median mortgaged household here pays about $1,405 a year in property tax; in the Austin metro it's about $7,403.

The questions people ask before they move here

Is Huntsville, Alabama a good place to live?

It depends on three things: whether your work is in the defence, space and engineering ecosystem, whether you need a specific school district, and whether a humid Southern summer and a car-dependent city suit you. We wrote the long version, including who should not move here.

The honest pros and cons

Where do most people live in Huntsville?

The west side — Madison, Harvest, Monrovia, Meridianville — for arsenal and Research Park jobs, because that is where the drive is shortest. The east and south — Five Points, Blossomwood, Hampton Cove, Owens Cross Roads — for downtown access, the hospital and more land.

Is Huntsville cheaper than where I live now?

Usually on house prices and property tax — the median mortgaged household here pays about $1,405 a year in property tax. One caveat in the other direction: Alabama levies a state income tax, so movers from Tennessee, Texas, Florida or Washington take home less per dollar of salary.
