What the count means. These are offences reported to and recorded by HPD under the FBI's National Incident-Based Reporting System — not incidents witnessed by police, and not victims. Reporting rates differ by offence type, which is why theft counts dwarf homicide counts everywhere and why year-to-year movement in larceny is a statement about reporting as much as about offending.

Per-resident arithmetic, for scale only. Against a 2024 population estimate of 230,157, the 13,725 total works out to about 59.6 reported offences per 1,000 residents, and the 892 violent offences to about 3.9 per 1,000. That is our division, not the department's, and it should not be used to rank Huntsville against other cities — city limits, reporting practice and category definitions all differ.

Since 2019 — the growth argument

The city's own framing, and the most useful single sentence in the release: as Huntsville's population grew 24% since 2019, violent crime fell 50% and overall major crime fell 33%.

Read that as the department reads it — growth has not come with a proportional rise in crime — and read it with the caveat that 2019 is a chosen base year, and that the city also describes itself as "one of the nation's safest," which is a claim rather than a measurement. What is measurable is the direction of travel in its own published counts.

Where the department is investing: the North Alabama Multi-Agency Crime Center (real-time video, plate readers, drones), 450+ sworn officers, a Co-Responder program with WellStone for mental-health calls, and Project Lifesaver for residents with cognitive conditions.

The part most "safest neighbourhoods" pages leave out. The same annual report that records the overall decline also flags that from January through June 2025 Huntsville saw a rise in reported car break-ins compared with the same period in 2024, including an increase in stolen firearms from vehicles — which is why HPD ran its "Lock, Secure, Protect" campaign. That detail matters if you are choosing between two houses on the basis of driveway parking, street lighting or whether there is a garage. It is also the kind of specific, unflattering fact that a listicle ranking neighbourhoods will never give you.

How to check one street before you buy

A citywide rate is an average over 230,000 people. You are buying one address. Six things actually tell you something:

1. Ask the precinct, not the internet

HPD runs three precincts — North, South and West — and each one assigns two Community Relations Officers as the primary point of contact for residents on quality-of-life and crime-prevention questions. That is a phone call to a person who covers your street, which no ranking can match.

2. Check the record for the parcel

HPD publishes reports and data through the city; the department's annual reports carry the category counts. Ask specifically about the reporting period, and ask whether the incidents were at an address or on the block.

3. Drive it at the hour you will live there

9pm on a Tuesday, not 11am on a Saturday. Look at lighting, how many cars are parked on the street, whether packages sit on porches, and whether the neighbouring properties are maintained. Most of what people call "the feel of an area" is observable in twenty minutes.

4. Ask about the neighbourhood watch

Huntsville's Community Watch program has run since 1979 and currently covers 150+ groups and more than 12,000 households, with each precinct's CROs supporting them. A street with an active group has a functioning channel to the department.

5. Register your cameras — or ask whether the street has

HPD's Security Camera Share Program is voluntary: residents and businesses register outward-facing cameras so investigators can request footage quickly after a burglary or vehicle break-in. It is a proxy for how engaged a street is, and it is a genuinely useful thing to sign up for after you move.

6. Price the insurance for the address

Two houses a mile apart can quote differently on both home and auto — theft and hail claims history are address-level, not city-level. Get quotes before you make an offer, not after. It is also the fastest way to find out something the seller will not mention.

Why we don't rank "the safest neighbourhoods"

Because we would be guessing. Street-level counts for an area of a few thousand people are small enough that one bad month, or one apartment complex, changes the ranking — and because the underlying data is offences reported to police, a low count can mean a quiet area or an area where people do not report. Safety questions, answered

Is Huntsville, Alabama a safe place to live?

By the department's own 2025 numbers, reported crime is falling while the city grows: total reported offences down 5.8%, violent crime down 18.3%, calls for service down 8%, with population up 24% since 2019 against a 50% fall in violent crime. The citywide picture is favourable. Your street still needs checking separately.

What is the crime rate in Huntsville?

HPD recorded 13,725 offences in 2025 across every NIBRS category, of which 892 were violent. Against a population estimate of 230,157 that is roughly 59.6 reported offences and 3.9 violent offences per 1,000 residents. We give the counts rather than a single headline rate because the counts are what the department publishes.

Which parts of Huntsville have the most crime?

We don't publish neighbourhood comparisons, and we'd be cautious about anyone who does — small-area counts move a lot month to month and reflect reporting as much as offending. The three precincts (North, South, West) are the department's own geography; for a specific block, ask that precinct's Community Relations Officers.

Does crime affect home insurance here?

It can, but wind and hail dominate North Alabama premiums — theft claims are usually the smaller part of the quote. What you should do is quote the actual address before making an offer. What to ask an insurer.

Is it safe to live outside the city limits?

Different agencies cover different addresses — Huntsville Police inside the city, the Madison County Sheriff's Office in unincorporated areas, and municipal departments in Madison, Athens, Decatur and the smaller towns. Which one responds to your address is a factual question worth asking before you buy, and it is often a surprise to people moving from a single-agency metro.

Where can I see the raw data?

The HPD Annual Report 2025 is the primary document for everything on this page. For cross-city comparison the FBI's Crime Data Explorer is the standard source — with the standing caveat that departments differ in what they report and how they count.
